## Proposed title

Mapping acupuncture-related research on spasm and spasticity: a dual-database bibliometric analysis with disease-stratified and data-completeness sensitivity analyses

## Introduction

Spasm and spasticity are used across heterogeneous clinical and experimental literatures. Muscle spasticity is associated with upper motor neuron disorders, whereas the broader term spasm encompasses other patterns of involuntary muscle contraction. This distinction matters when mapping acupuncture-related research: studies of post-stroke limb spasticity, hemifacial spasm and other spasm-related conditions do not necessarily address the same disease mechanisms or therapeutic objectives. A literature map should therefore retain the breadth of the search while identifying the clinical domains represented within that map [1,2].

Acupuncture-related interventions also encompass different techniques and research traditions. Manual acupuncture, electroacupuncture, moxibustion, fire needling and dry needling may be investigated in different patient populations and through different study designs. A pooled description of publication output can identify prominent terms and contributors, but cannot establish that these interventions are interchangeable or that a frequently studied intervention is clinically superior. In this setting, bibliometric analysis is most useful for describing the organization and development of the literature and for identifying questions that require subsequent clinical or mechanistic investigation.

Several adjacent research landscapes have already been mapped. A bibliometric study of acupuncture for stroke described publications across a broad rehabilitation field [3]. A separate analysis examined non-pharmacological therapies for post-stroke spastic paralysis [4]. More recently, a study combined bibliometric analysis with meta-analysis specifically for acupuncture in post-stroke spastic hemiplegia [5], while another examined dry needling for pain using Web of Science Core Collection and Scopus [6]. These studies establish that publication growth, productive contributors and keyword patterns in related fields are already documented. An updated map therefore requires an explicit contribution beyond extending the search date or presenting additional network figures.

The present study is designed to examine that contribution in two ways. First, it will distinguish the disease domains and intervention themes contained within the broad spasm/spasticity literature, allowing the overall map to be interpreted alongside disease-specific subsets. Second, it will evaluate the extent to which database coverage and bibliographic completeness influence the observed publication and collaboration patterns. This is particularly relevant when inclusion requires complete author, affiliation and keyword information: such a requirement may change the composition of the literature being represented, rather than merely improve the appearance of the network.

Accordingly, the study will combine records from Web of Science Core Collection and PubMed, retain the provenance of matched and database-specific records, and examine publication trends, collaboration networks, source journals and keyword development. Keyword co-occurrence, clustering, temporal visualization and burst detection will be interpreted alongside disease and intervention classifications. Sensitivity analyses will examine the consequences of search expansion and bibliographic completeness. Transparent reporting of the executed searches and record flow will be informed by PRISMA-S [7]. The purpose is to characterize the literature and the robustness of its representation, rather than to infer treatment effectiveness from bibliometric prominence.

## Contribution statement for eventual cover letter

This study addresses how disease scope, database coverage and bibliographic completeness shape the interpretation of research on acupuncture-related interventions for spasm and spasticity. Its intended contribution is a disease-stratified map accompanied by explicit provenance and sensitivity analyses. This statement should be retained only if these analyses are completed and supported by the final results.
